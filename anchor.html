AR Reference Anchor Test

Tests whether a virtual reference object — rendered into the camera view and locked to a real-world surface via WebXR hit-test — actually stays put and sized correctly at the distance you'd capture a product from. No physical card needed for this test; you're watching whether the anchor itself is trustworthy.

What you'll see

  1. Point the phone at a table or flat surface. A wireframe rectangle sized to a standard card (85.60 × 53.98 mm) will appear where the surface is detected, updating live.
  2. Tap Lock reference here once it looks stable. The rectangle then stays fixed at that world position — it no longer follows your aim.
  3. Move the phone side to side and toward/away from it. A real anchor holds still against the real surface; if it slides, swims, or changes size as you move, the anchoring isn't reliable at that distance.
  4. Start at arm's length (50–100 cm) where tracking is easiest, then slowly move closer to your actual product-capture distance and watch when (or if) it breaks down.

Why this matters

If the anchor holds steady and correctly sized at your real capture distance, a virtual reference object could work without a physical card. If it drifts or misjudges size at that distance — consistent with the depth and hit-test readings already measured on this device — that confirms the physical reference-object approach should stay the production path.
